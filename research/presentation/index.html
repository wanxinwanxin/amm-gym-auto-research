AMM-gym auto-research

A self-contained narrative built up cycle by cycle. Last updated 2026-04-30 (cycle 1). Read top-to-bottom; later milestones build on earlier ones.

Project in one paragraph. We have a step-by-step AMM market-making simulator with two flavors — a synthetic "challenge" mode used as a scoring benchmark, and a "realistic" mode whose price process and retail flow are fitted from on-chain ETH/USDC data and Binance ETHUSDT order-book data. Four research questions: (M1) does the realistic mode actually look like the chain? (M2) can we beat the benchmark scoring rule? (M3) does training in the easy mode generalize to the realistic mode? (M4) how does optimal realistic performance scale with policy complexity?

M1 — Validating the realistic simulator against on-chain markout

Status: starting. Cycle 1 inspected the BigQuery ground-truth schema, ran a one-day percentile snapshot for the canonical reference pool, and laid out the cycle 2 plan. No simulator runs yet.

Ground-truth reference

The canonical reference is the WETH/USDC 0.05% Uniswap v3 pool on Ethereum (0x88e6a0c2...5640) — the same pool the realistic simulator's price-return and retail-impact artifacts were fitted from. The on-chain markout column we'll compare against is uniswap-labs.research.markout_prod.markout_next (log-return between the next-block reference mid and the trade's executed price).

The mean is positive (LPs net-collect) and the body is reasonably tight. The left tail is fatter than the right (-73 bps vs +14 bps at the 1st/99th percentile) — that's the classic adverse-selection signature of toxic flow occasionally arriving and getting picked off.

Plan for cycle 2

Run the realistic simulator with fixed-fee 30 bps strategies on both venues (so we measure pure dynamics, not policy effects), dump every retail trade with a reconstructed markout, and overlay the percentile table and CDF against this ground truth. Identify which percentiles match and which don't, then form a hypothesis about the divergence (price tape, retail size distribution, arb model, fee structure).
